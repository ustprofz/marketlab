# Make finance feel like an investigation

The first version emphasizes familiar brands, short questions, and evidence rather than a dense professional trading terminal. All ideas below are proposals unless marked included.

## Included now

- **Start with something students know.** Eight starter tickers connect everyday brands to company data. Students can search additional instruments in Yahoo mode.
- **Make percentages concrete.** A historical $100 illustration links a chart to proportional reasoning, with fees/dividends/taxes limitations visible.
- **Be a data detective.** Students identify the source, timestamp, units, and price field before interpreting a result.
- **Predict, compare, explain.** Indexed charts, return scatterplots, and histograms support a claim–evidence–limitation investigation.
- **Give teachers a fast starting point.** Three short lesson plans, a printable guide, activity-sheet export, and a suggested 10-point rubric are ready to use.
- **Make lessons repeatable.** Simulated practice data, CSV snapshots, shareable settings, and local CSV import help teachers prepare for unreliable classroom networks.

## Useful next additions

| Idea | Student appeal | Teacher value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minnesota business missions | Investigate familiar local businesses and products. | Connect business, geography, and community examples; distinguish public from private companies. |
| “Guess, then reveal” challenges | Predict a chart's percentage change or which return series varies more. | A quick formative check with reasoning prompts instead of profit rankings. |
| Teacher-owned frozen datasets | Every group begins with the same dated snapshot and prompt. | Reproducible marking; add this only with appropriate provider/data rights. |
| Event annotations | Attach a sourced earnings date or news event to a chart. | Practice source evaluation; explain why timing alone does not establish causality. |
| AP Statistics extension | Explore sampling windows, outliers, bootstraps, and uncertainty. | Connect descriptive statistics to inference while discussing dependent financial observations. |
| Spreadsheet/Python bridges | Complete the same calculation in Sheets, Excel, or a notebook. | Build transferable data-cleaning and reproducible-analysis skills. |
| Classroom challenge cards | Earn completion badges for identifying missing data or correcting a misleading chart. | Reward evidence and explanation rather than speculative trading gains. |
| Student-designed investigations | Ask whether brands in different sectors tend to move together. | Small group projects with a written claim, reproducible dataset, and peer feedback. |

## Operational priorities before a wider school rollout

Test with a small group of teachers and students, including keyboard and screen-reader users. Ask where labels, percentages, or source modes are confusing. Use their feedback to simplify the first five minutes of the experience.

For a larger rollout, decide on a licensed market-data arrangement, stronger shared caching and rate controls, uptime monitoring, and a classroom support plan. Accounts and classroom analytics are not part of the current design; add them only if their educational value justifies the additional privacy and operational work.
