Market Lab · Teacher guide · Suggested grades 9–12

Teach finance, descriptive statistics, and data analytics through short investigations. Students need a browser and curiosity; no student account is required. Use your browser’s Print command to print or save this page as a PDF.

Open teacher tools →Open practice data →

Choose the source before teaching. Practice mode is a fixed, simulated dataset with recognizable ticker labels; it is not real company performance. Yahoo mode uses a separate yfinance service and may be delayed or unavailable. Check source, currency, market observation date, and price basis together. A recent retrieval time does not mean a recent market trade.

Prepare in five minutes

  1. Open Market Lab and choose Practice data for a repeatable lesson. For actual market data, the site owner must deploy and configure the included Python service.
  2. Choose AAPL, the 1Y window, and Close. For comparison, choose MSFT in the Data Lab. Keep source, window, and price basis consistent across the class.
  3. Download the CSV before class if you want a fixed dataset. Practice CSVs are labelled SIMULATED. User-imported CSVs are labelled user-imported.
  4. Open “Data sources, timing, and calculation notes.” Review the difference between Close, Adjusted Close, and a market quote.

Three classroom investigations

1 · From a familiar brand to a financial chart · 10–15 minutes

Question: What can one share price tell us—and what does it leave out?

  1. Choose a familiar company and identify its ticker, reported currency, source, and observation date.
  2. Read first and last prices. Calculate (last ÷ first − 1) × 100. Compare with the selected-range change.
  3. Try the $100 illustration. Explain why it is a historical price-change example rather than a forecast or a complete investment return.
  4. Write one thing the chart alone cannot establish. A higher share price alone does not establish a larger company; shares outstanding also matter.

2 · Price is a level; return is a change · 15–20 minutes

Question: Can two observations summarize a whole distribution?

  1. Download a CSV. Calculate a few consecutive returns: new price ÷ previous price − 1.
  2. Use the Data Lab histogram to describe the center, spread, and unusual observations. Compare mean and median using a spreadsheet if desired.
  3. Switch between 6, 12, and 20 bins. Explain why the histogram’s appearance changes while the underlying observations do not.
  4. Interpret sample standard deviation in percentage points per return interval. The site reports per-step sample SD, not an annualized risk measure.

3 · Different prices, a fair comparison · 20–25 minutes

Question: How can we compare two companies that start with different share prices?

  1. Predict how AAPL and MSFT might move together, then open the Data Lab.
  2. Compare indexed prices: each starts at 100 on the first shared timestamp. Describe the difference in percentage growth.
  3. Inspect the scatterplot and Pearson correlation of matched returns. Correlation describes association; it does not establish causation.
  4. Change the time window. Write a claim with two numerical pieces of evidence and one limitation. Save it with “Download investigation.”

Suggested total: 10 points. Assess reasoning rather than investment gains. These activities provide practice with descriptive statistics; they are not sufficient on their own for statistical inference or a trading strategy evaluation.

Practical notes

  • Yahoo charts use regular trading hours; weekends and market holidays can leave the latest observation on an earlier date. Availability and delay vary by instrument and provider.
  • Close and Adjusted Close are distinct fields. Missing or nonpositive selected prices are excluded from analytics, while available raw rows remain in the CSV.
  • Pairwise comparisons align shared timestamps. Consecutive shared observations may span gaps. Correlation uses returns, not price levels.
  • Import accepts up to 6,000 rows and 2 MB with Date/Timestamp and Close columns. It stays in the browser. The first version supports one imported series at a time.
  • Share links record settings, not a frozen dataset or student notes. Download a CSV to preserve a snapshot.
  • For Excel, use Data → From Text/CSV if dates or decimals are misread. For Google Sheets, use File → Import. Return is =B3/B2-1 when prices are in column B; sample variability uses STDEV.S.
  • yfinance is independent of Yahoo. The operator must review provider and library guidance and any data-use rights before public redistribution.
